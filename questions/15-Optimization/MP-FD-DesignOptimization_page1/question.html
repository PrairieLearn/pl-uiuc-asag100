Design Optimization: Introduction

Topology optimization is a mathematical method that optimizes material distribution within a given design space for a given set of loads, boundary conditions, and constraints with the goal of maximizing the performance of the system. For example, we can use topology optimization methods to design bridge structures that are structurally safe and use a reduced amount of material. In this MP, you will write part of a topology optimization algorithm to design a simple bridge like the one in the figure below. For simplicity, you will model the bridge as a two-dimensional problem (think of modeling only one side of the bridge) and also assume symmetry, so that your final design will look something like the figure on the right.

To accomplish this, let us consider a rectangular box (like the one indicated below) that is constrained from horizontal movement on the left side and from vertical movement on the bottom right corner. Under these movement constraints, a load is then applied to the block at the top left corner. From now on, we will call the rectangular box the design space.

Our goal is to find the best distribution of material inside the design space that gives us the "stronger" structure. The natural thing to do is formulate this as an optimization problem and solve it! We will walk you through the steps to solve this optimization problem. First, we need to create a uniform grid of unit sized squares representing the material inside the rectangular design space. We will call each square an "element". In this example, the rectangular domain has dimensions 4 by 2 and a total of 8 square elements. Here we will denote nelx the number of elements in the x-direction, nely the number of elements in the y-direction, and hence nel = nelx*nely is the total number of elements. Note here nelx = 4 and nely = 2.

We define a design variable $x$ for each square element, where $x = 1$ means the element is filled with material (represented by a square with black color), and $x = 0$ means the element is not filled with material (represented by a square with white color). In practice, the design variable is a continuous function that can assume values in between `0` and `1` (square in gray), but the implementation of the algorithm favors the extreme values. The optimization question here becomes: what is the best distribition of $0s$ and $1s$ inside the rectangular domain that maximizes the stiffness of the structure? Mathematically, the optimization problem is posed as:

$$ \max_{ {\bf x} } S({\bf x}) $$

where ${\bf x}$ is the vector with the design variables corresponding to the material distribution, and $S({\bf x})$ is the stiffness of the structure.

Let's say that we only have enough material to fill half of the design domain. This can be posed as a constraint in the optimization problem. The "amount" of material can be represented by the summation of all the design variables. As such, we can rewrite the optimization problem as:

$$ \max_{ {\bf x} } S({\bf x}) s.t. \sum x_i <= 0.5 (nelx*nely)$$ The animation above shows the optimization steps, wherein material is distributed inside the design space under these given constraints.
