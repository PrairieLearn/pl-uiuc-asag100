Solving $v(t)=0$ gives $t={{correct_answers.turn_time}}\,\mathrm{s}$. Velocity changes from {{params.initial_direction}} to {{params.final_direction}} at this time, so the particle reverses direction.

Displacement is the signed integral of velocity: \[ \int_0^{ {{params.end_time}} } ({{params.velocity}})\,dt = {{correct_answers.displacement}}\,\mathrm{m}. \]

The signed displacements before and after the turn are \[ \int_0^{ {{correct_answers.turn_time}} } ({{params.velocity}})\,dt = {{correct_answers.first_displacement}}\,\mathrm{m}, \qquad \int_{ {{correct_answers.turn_time}} }^{ {{params.end_time}} } ({{params.velocity}})\,dt = {{correct_answers.second_displacement}}\,\mathrm{m}. \] Adding their absolute values gives a total distance of ${{correct_answers.distance}}\,\mathrm{m}$.

Displacement accounts for direction, while distance counts all travel as positive. Look for a justified sign change, correct integrals, units, and an explanation of this distinction.
